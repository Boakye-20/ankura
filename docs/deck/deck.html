AI Strategy · the client problem and why this approach

Meadowlane Bank's complaints are slow because handlers search and re-type, not because they decide badly. So the AI assists the handler and never decides.

The problem

Meadowlane Bank plc (fictional UK high-street retail bank) has a growing complaints backlog against FCA deadlines: 15 business days for payment complaints, 8 weeks for the rest. Handlers spend much of each case finding the right policy clause and writing from scratch. Specialist teams return incomplete handovers, adding days to time-limited cases.

Why assist, not automate

Outcome decisions carry Consumer Duty, Ombudsman and UK GDPR Article 22A exposure, and "agentic AI will do broken work faster". We redesigned the handover (a pack that cannot be sent incomplete) rather than only speeding up drafting. A named person approves, edits or escalates every case, with a reason.

Scope

In: masking, triage, policy search, cited draft, checks, handover pack, delay letters, audit trail, adoption measures. Out: sending anything, live model calls in the demo, real customer data, root-cause analytics (roadmap).

Assumptions to test in a pilot

  • Time is lost in search and typing, not in judgement (time handlers with and without the tool).
  • Specialist returns are caused by missing information the pack can capture.
  • A 13-check gate is acceptable friction: 17 of 60 stored drafts are locked for a real reason today.

How it was built and how AI tools were used

Six steps: four in code, two with a model. Code does everything that must be the same every time; the model does the reading and writing.

1 · CodeMaskNames, accounts, contacts and merchants become tokens. A leak check stops the case if anything slips through.
2 · AI model (Claude Haiku)TriageType, key facts, signs of vulnerability, which deadline applies. Structured output, validated by code.
3 · CodeFind policyKeyword (BM25) search over 87 clauses in 11 policies. Deadline, ombudsman and redress clauses always included.
4 · AI model (Claude Sonnet)DraftA reply with the policy clause behind each claim, and a handover pack when a specialist team is needed.
5 · CodeCheck13 checks: masking, injection, citations exist and cover claims, redress limits, ombudsman wording, plain language.
6 · PersonDecideApprove, edit and re-check, or escalate, with a reason. Vulnerable customers and higher redress need a senior too.

Design choices

  • Keyword search, not embeddings: deterministic and explainable for about 100 clauses; a pilot would test hybrid retrieval.
  • Controls in code, not in the prompt: a prompt can be talked out of a rule; a check cannot, and it writes its own evidence.
  • Technology-agnostic: models are set by configuration, so a bank can swap in its own.
  • Stored outputs for the demo: the hosted app serves a real model run from 09/11/2026 and never calls a model, so assessors need no key and all see the same results.
  • Plain English for non-technical staff: policy references read "Unauthorised transactions and fraud, section 3.3"; statuses read "Waiting for a senior".

Cost £0.0094 per complaint at list prices (Batch API, half price). Time 7.5 s per case average, 10.7 s at the 95th percentile (from the non-batched run). Four runs are kept because the model does not answer the same way twice.

The three adversarial misses, and what the code did

  • Invented policy (2 cases): the complaint cited "policy 9.4"; the model offered £180 and £500 on its strength. The claims-backed-by-policy check locked one draft; the injection check withheld the other. No amount reached a customer.
  • Off-topic complaint (1 case): a mortgage query was sorted as Lending with confidence 0.60. The confidence check referred it to a person.

Most instructive failures

  • Six routing misses are cases with two signs of vulnerability sent to the Vulnerability Team rather than a senior: a labelling rule I would revisit with the bank.
  • Five deadline misses are summary-resolution calls: the "resolved within three days" rule needs clearer case notes.
  • The model's own confidence separates right from wrong only weakly (43/45 right above 0.75, 11/15 below), so it is not shown to handlers.

Limitations

One person wrote and labelled all 60 cases, so each case is 1.7 points. Prompt v2 was tuned after seeing v1's errors on the same cases: no held-out set, so new complaints would score lower. The citation rule was corrected twice after this evaluation; stored checks were re-run by code, the evaluation was not. Masking is rules and name lists. No real handler has used it. A pilot must prove real data quality, integration, calibration, staff behaviour and cost at volume, against a control team: a pilot designed to succeed will not scale.

What the FCA's AI report (October 2026) asked for

The report's areas for improvement include over-reliance on "a human in the loop" and assuming a model can spot vulnerability without testing it. Here the human step is designed (locked approval, reasons, the 20-second prompt) and vulnerability detection is measured (14 of 14 spotted, 2 false alarms in 16).

Monitoring in production

Drift in the mix of complaint types; citation failures per week; override and amendment rates; handover returns; a monthly QA sample read by a senior; re-run the evaluation after any prompt, policy or model change. Horizon: FCA redress reforms (CP26/9) and the ICO statutory AI code.

AI Workforce Transformation · people, value and roadmap

Handlers move from searching and typing to checking and deciding. Adoption is measured inside the product, so quiet reversion shows early.

SpecialistChases missing informationReceives the same complete pack every time; returns count
QA and RiskPeriodic file reviewsLive evidence from the audit trail and results page

Skills and change

AI literacy; judging a draft against the complaint; checking a policy reference; recording a reason. Workflow co-designed with handlers in the pilot; partners such as Decoded for literacy and Seymour Powell for the handler experience.

Adoption measures built in

  • Share of drafts approved unchanged versus edited (rubber-stamping or distrust)
  • Approvals under 20 seconds and prompts confirmed
  • Escalations and handovers returned by specialists
  • Reason codes chosen; time from opening a case to a decision

Value case (every input an assumption to test)

Minutes saved per case × complaints per year × loaded cost per hour, plus fewer returned handovers and fewer missed deadlines. AI cost about 1p per complaint. Framed as capacity to clear the backlog, not headcount. The case fails if review time eats the saving, which is why handling time is measured against a control team.
